## Module 04: main effects

All 117 files exist and match their recorded checksums. The analysis is not
publication-ready.

### Primary interpretation

- All six CCPP-equal primary first stages pass the registered Kleibergen--Paap
  threshold of strictly greater than 10. The 2013 values are only about
  10.49--11.10; the 2017 values are about 20.89--54.37.
- None of the 48 primary fuzzy-LATE estimates survives Holm or BH correction.
- In the registered eight-outcome complete-case sample, the only adjusted
  primary result is the 2017 individual assignment reduced
  form for living in another CCPP: **+43.72 percentage points**, 95% CI
  **[13.87, 73.56]**, raw p = .0041, Holm/BH p = .0327.
- The corresponding treatment-receipt LATE is **+45.62 points**, 95% CI
  **[-0.40, 91.64]**, raw p = .052, Holm p = .416. It is suggestive rather
  than confirmatory.
- A migration-specific linked-adult sample that does not require 2017
  wellbeing completeness changes the assignment reduced form to **+20.90**
  points (raw p = .118) and the fuzzy estimate to **+21.86** points (raw
  p = .270), despite a Kleibergen--Paap F of **20.66**. The registered
  migration result must not be described as an effect for all linked adults.
- Correcting the common denominator for extreme non-linkage codings yields
  fuzzy estimates of **+7.84** and **+12.46** points; both intervals include
  zero. These are alternative outcome codings in a broader population,
  not formal bounds or directly comparable estimates for one target population.
- The 2017 CCPP employment estimate is directionally stable, roughly -14 to
  -21 points, but its main estimate does not survive multiplicity correction
  (Holm p about .458).
- Household- and person-equal sensitivity estimands have weak first stages in
  both waves and must not be interpreted causally.

District-capital status lacks adequate local binary-cell support in every

## Module 06: migration and mechanism evidence

The mechanism hierarchy is coherent only if its labels remain strict:

- Migration reduced forms and fuzzy LATEs are outcome evidence.
- Linkage analysis is a selection diagnostic.
- Employment, connectivity, and related treatment effects are candidate
  intermediate outcomes, not causal mediators.
- Cross-outcome associations are descriptive only.

The registered complete-case linked-person assignment effect is
adjusted-significant, but the LATE is not. The reduced form becomes
non-significant when migration-observed adults are no longer excluded for
missing wellbeing data. The linkage discontinuity is about -8.28 points
with a wide interval, so failure to reject does not establish
missing-at-random linkage.

No candidate intermediate-outcome effect survives Holm or BH adjustment. The
clearest raw pattern is lower 2017 CCPP employment, about -14.14 points with
raw p about .057 and Holm p about .458. The adjusted descriptive association
between 2013 employment and 2017 migration is +6.73 points per one-standard-
deviation difference (95% CI [2.14, 11.32], BH q = .015) in only 59 CCPPs and
41 clusters. It remains noncausal.

Do not report ACME, ADE, NIE, NDE, natural direct or indirect effects,
proportion mediated, or a product-of-coefficients interpretation. Current data
do not identify these estimands.

## Literature-grounded interpretation

NotebookLM was consulted through the project URL for source discovery,
despite a contradictory authentication-health flag. Its generated synthesis
was not treated as source evidence. Zotero library `IE Collective Reparations
Peru` was accessed read-only, and underlying attachments were checked where
searchable.

The verified sources support the following boundaries:

- Laplante and Theidon (2007; Zotero item 1675) ground Peru reparations in
  acknowledgment, rights, and redress amid delayed and contested
  implementation. They do not establish this program's causal effects.
- Firchow (2013, item 1690; 2014, item 1687) documents the tension between
  reparations and ordinary development, as well as the importance of
  implementation, participation, accompaniment, and attribution. This makes
  project type theoretically relevant but not exogenous.
- Moffett (2015, item 1672) distinguishes corrective reparation from broader
  development and warns against loading reparations with structural-policy
  goals. The Peru PIR is not simply an antipoverty program.
- Adhikari and Gentilini (2018, item 1794), Selod and Shilpi (2021, item 1789),
  and Lagakos et al. (2023, item 1792) support competing anchoring and mobility-
  facilitation pathways. They do not determine the sign of the Peru effect.
- Guarin et al. (item 1756) offer comparative Colombian evidence for individual
  cash reparations. Its treatment, unit, and institutional setting differ from
  Peru's community projects, so coefficients and mechanisms cannot be
  transported.

Zotero returned full searchable passages for these substantive sources. The
Bartalotti--Brummet clustered-RD attachment (item 1652) was abstract-only and
the Gelman--Imbens polynomial attachment (item 1654) metadata-only. Their full
texts must be inspected through an approved reader before they support a
manuscript sentence.

## Allowed and prohibited claims

Allowed, with qualifications:

- in the registered wellbeing-complete linked-adult subset, the assignment
  reduced form for CCPP migration was positive and passed its original
  multiplicity adjustment, but did not persist in a migration-specific
  linked-adult sample;
- the corresponding treatment-receipt migration LATE is positive but
  imprecise and not multiplicity-robust;
- alternative non-linkage codings are imprecise and use a different source-
  cohort population, not formal effect bounds;
- no multiplicity-robust treatment effect on candidate intermediate outcomes
  or registered heterogeneity interaction was found; and
- baseline employment is positively associated with later migration in a
  small CCPP sample.

Prohibited:

- reparations definitively caused a 45.6-point migration increase;
- there is no linkage selection or linkage is missing at random;
- employment or internet access mediates the treatment effect;
- project type or per-capita financing causally explains migration;
- `rdhte` validates fuzzy-LATE heterogeneity;
- the CCPP, household, and individual estimates are independent replications;
  or
- null adjusted results prove no effect.
