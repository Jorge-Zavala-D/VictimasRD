# Migration, Linkage, and Mechanism Analysis Protocol

**Project:** Victimas RD

**Version:** 2026-09-22

**Status:** Pending user review; no separate research-team approval gate is required

**Applies to:** `06_analyze_migration_mechanisms.do`, `07_build_tables_figures.do`, and `08_run_release_checks.do`

## 1. Purpose

This protocol governs the migration, linkage-selection, and exploratory
mechanism workflow that follows the main-effect and heterogeneity analyses. It
preserves the fuzzy regression-discontinuity design already fixed in
`docs/RD_OUTCOME_ANALYSIS_PROTOCOL.md` while preventing descriptive mechanism
evidence from being mislabeled as causal mediation.

The workflow must answer four distinct questions:

1. Does collective-reparation receipt affect migration outcomes near the B/C
   cutoff?
2. Does assignment or treatment affect whether a SISFOH source-cohort person
   is linked to the 2017 Census?
3. Does treatment affect outcomes that are substantively relevant to proposed
   mechanisms, including connectivity and labor-market measures?
4. Are those mechanism measures associated with migration in the observed
   data?

Only the first three questions can inherit causal RD interpretations. The
fourth is descriptive. The available data do not identify natural direct or
indirect effects.

## 2. Scope and non-goals

### In scope

- Reuse and validate the aggregate estimates generated by the six module-04
  outcome programs rather than re-estimating identical models.
- Consolidate 2017 migration effects at the CCPP, source-household, and person
  levels.
- Diagnose Census-linkage selection using the complete 2013 source cohort.
- Present extreme-case non-linkage codings as sensitivity endpoints.
- Consolidate treatment effects on 2013 and 2017 connectivity and labor
  outcomes as evidence about possible channels.
- Estimate clearly labeled, noncausal associations between temporally ordered
  2013 CCPP mechanism measures and 2017 CCPP migration.
- Create disclosure-safe aggregate tables, figures, and output manifests.
- Build a publication-candidate inventory and a release audit without copying
  unreviewed outputs to Overleaf.

### Out of scope

- Natural, controlled, or interventional direct and indirect effects.
- Product-of-coefficients, proportion-mediated, or Sobel-style claims.
- Treating 2017 internet access or employment as a mediator of migration that
  occurred between 2013 and 2017.
- Using post-treatment variables as ordinary controls in total-effect models.
- Treating non-linkage as observed migration in the primary analysis.
- Project-type heterogeneity or financing-dose analysis, which belongs to the
  completed heterogeneity workflow.
- New raw-data intake, new record linkage, or any write to Dropbox Raw.
- Automatic publication, manuscript editing, or Overleaf synchronization.

## 3. Inherited RD design contract

Module 06 must not redefine the core design. It inherits the following fixed
choices from the approved outcome protocol:

| Element | Contract |
|---|---|
| Geographic sample | Selected legacy RD geography, encoded by `rd_bc_design` |
| Policy comparison | Adjacent B/C victimization categories |
| Running variable | `running_bc`, centered at zero |
| 2013 treatment | `treat_12` |
| 2017 treatment | `treat_16` |
| Assignment instrument | Indicator for crossing the B/C cutoff |
| Primary estimator | Local-linear fuzzy RD with robust bias-corrected inference |
| Common estimation bandwidth | `h = 0.0075` |
| Bias bandwidth | `b = 0.0135` |
| Kernel | Triangular |
| Weak-instrument gate | Conditional first-stage F must exceed 10 |
| CCPP inference | District-clustered primary inference |
| Household/person weighting | CCPP-equal primary weights |
| Household/person inference | CCPP-clustered primary inference |
| Primary estimate | Fuzzy-RD LATE, conditional on the instrument gate |
| Supporting estimates | Assignment reduced form and parametric local 2SLS |

The common bandwidth is a design bandwidth selected independently of
substantive outcomes. Outcome-specific bandwidths remain sensitivity analyses
and cannot replace the common-window primary results.

## 4. Timing and causal roles

The relevant observed sequence is:

1. Predetermined geography, victimization score, 2007 Census covariates, and
   other baseline measures.
2. B/C cutoff assignment.
3. Collective-reparation receipt through 2012 or 2016.
4. SISFOH 2013 connectivity and labor measures.
5. Migration between the 2013 source location and the 2017 Census location.
6. 2017 destination-household internet access, employment, and household
   conditions.

This timing has three consequences:

- The 2013 measures precede most of the measured migration interval and are
  eligible as *candidate intermediate variables*, but noncompliance,
  mediator-outcome confounding, later treatment, and linkage selection prevent
  a causal indirect-effect interpretation without stronger assumptions.
- The 2017 measures are outcomes of treatment and useful evidence about
  channels, but they are contemporaneous with or downstream of migration.
  They cannot be mediators of the same 2013--2017 migration outcome.
- No model may condition on 2013 or 2017 mechanism measures when estimating
  the total effect of treatment on migration.

## 5. Populations, outcomes, and estimands

### 5.1 Target populations

The broad target population is victimized Peruvian CCPPs in the selected RD
geography whose B/C scores lie within the common window. Each analytical level
then uses its validated source cohort:

- **CCPP:** represented RUV communities with the outcome-specific valid
  denominator.
- **Household:** 2013 SISFOH source households represented in the linked 2017
  construction, weighted so each source CCPP contributes equally.
- **Person:** members of the complete SISFOH source cohort for linkage analyses;
  linked, item-valid people for observed migration and 2017 outcomes, weighted
  so each source CCPP contributes equally.

Every table row must state its denominator. Complete-case samples for different
outcomes must not be described as a single common sample unless they are
identical by construction and verified in code.

### 5.2 Primary migration outcome

The primary migration concept is residence in a different CCPP in 2017 than
the canonical 2013 source CCPP:

- CCPP: `cpv2017_share_moved_ccpp`
- Household: `hh_share_moved_ccpp_2017`
- Person: `moved_ccpp_2013_2017`

The person-level outcome is the most direct measure. Household and CCPP
aggregates describe different estimands and are triangulation, not duplicate
replications.

### 5.3 Secondary migration outcomes

Secondary outcomes are moves across district, province, or department;
five-year retrospective mobility; source-household splitting across
destinations; and current-household reports of a member abroad. These remain
within their existing `migration_2017_*` multiplicity families.

### 5.4 Linkage-selection outcomes

The complete source-cohort linkage outcomes are `census2017_linked` and its
complement `census2017_not_linked`. At CCPP level, `cpv2017_link_rate` is the
validated aggregate diagnostic. Linkage is an outcome in its own right and is
never silently converted into a migration value.

### 5.5 Mechanism-relevant outcomes

The mechanism evidence uses only variables already approved in the outcome
registries:

- 2013 connectivity: `share_asset_internet_2013`,
  `wellbeing_connectivity_2013`, `asset_internet_2013`, and
  `asset_cable_2013`.
- 2013 labor: labor-force participation, employment, unemployment, child
  employment, and independent work, using the level-specific validated
  denominators.
- 2017 connectivity: `cpv2017_share_internet`,
  `wellbeing_connectivity_2017`, `hh_share_internet_2017`,
  `hh_single_dest_internet_2017`, `hh_wellbeing_connect_2017`,
  `internet_2017`, and the person-level connectivity domain.
- 2017 labor: employment, labor-force participation, and unemployment at each
  supported analytical level.
- Household dispersion: source-household splitting and household-member-abroad
  indicators, classified as migration-related outcomes rather than mediators.

### 5.6 Estimands

Each result must use exactly one of these labels:

1. **Assignment effect:** the discontinuity in an outcome at the B/C cutoff.
2. **Fuzzy-RD LATE:** the local effect of treatment receipt for treatment-status
   compliers near the cutoff, reported only when the first-stage gate passes.
3. **Parametric local 2SLS:** a robustness analogue estimated in the fixed
   common window.
4. **Selection sensitivity:** a noncausal comparison under an explicit
   non-linkage coding rule.
5. **Descriptive association:** an observed relationship between a candidate
   mechanism and migration, with no indirect-effect interpretation.
6. **Not identified:** a requested causal mediation quantity that the current
   data and assumptions cannot identify.

## 6. Evidence hierarchy

### Tier 1: Total migration effects

Module 06 extracts the common-window fuzzy LATE, assignment reduced form, and
parametric local 2SLS estimates already generated by module 04. The individual
CCPP-move outcome is the primary migration result. Household and CCPP estimates
are supporting evidence. No estimate may be recomputed with a different sample
merely to improve precision or significance.

### Tier 2: Linkage-selection analysis

The module must report:

- the cutoff discontinuity in person-level linkage;
- the cutoff discontinuity in the CCPP linkage rate;
- observed migration among linked people;
- the existing sensitivity outcome that codes every unlinked person as a
  mover; and
- the existing sensitivity outcome that codes every unlinked person as a
  non-mover.

The last two estimates are extreme-case coding endpoints, not formal sharp
bounds on a fuzzy-RD LATE. They must never be labeled as observed migration.
A linkage discontinuity close to zero reduces one specific concern but does not
establish missing-at-random or eliminate selection bias.

### Tier 3: Effects on mechanism-relevant outcomes

Treatment effects on connectivity and labor outcomes are causal fuzzy-RD
outcome effects when the inherited design and first-stage gate are satisfied.
They show whether treatment shifted a proposed channel. They do not show that
the channel caused migration or quantify an indirect effect.

### Tier 4: Descriptive mechanism associations

The only new mediator-migration association models will use CCPP-level 2013
mechanism measures and the 2017 CCPP migration outcome in the selected common
window. They will report:

- the unadjusted association;
- an association adjusted only for the approved predetermined covariates; and
- sample size, CCPP count, district-clustered uncertainty, and a clear
  `descriptive_association` label.

These regressions cannot include treatment-induced covariates, cannot be
multiplied by treatment-to-mechanism coefficients, and cannot be described as
direct, indirect, or mediated effects.

### Tier 5: Causal mediation gate

Causal mediation remains disabled unless a future protocol documents all of
the following:

1. a mediator measured after the relevant treatment and before the outcome;
2. a defined direct/indirect estimand for the fuzzy-RD complier population;
3. an identification strategy for mediator-outcome confounding;
4. explicit treatment of exposure-induced mediator-outcome confounders;
5. explicit treatment of Census linkage selection;
6. justified noncompliance assumptions and any additional instrument required
   by the chosen method;
7. a sensitivity analysis matched to that estimand; and
8. a separate, approved protocol and test contract.

Until all eight conditions are satisfied, module 06 must produce no ACME, ADE,
NIE, NDE, proportion-mediated, or product-of-coefficients estimate. The legacy
mediation code is historical evidence only.

## 7. Multiplicity and reporting hierarchy

Multiplicity follows the existing outcome registries rather than creating a
second set of families:

- primary migration outcomes retain their level-specific primary family;
- secondary migration outcomes retain `migration_2017_*`;
- linkage outcomes form a separate `linkage_2017` family;
- 2013 and 2017 connectivity outcomes remain separate by wave and level;
- labor outcomes remain separate by wave and level; and
- descriptive associations form one exploratory `mechanism_association`
  family.

Holm-adjusted p-values govern primary confirmatory families. Benjamini--Hochberg
q-values accompany secondary and mechanism families. Raw p-values remain
visible. Statistical significance cannot change an evidence-class label.

The main paper should prioritize, in order: the individual primary migration
effect, the linkage diagnostic and non-linkage sensitivity endpoints, then the
compact mechanism-outcome summary. Secondary migration and descriptive
associations belong in the appendix unless the manuscript's argument requires
one prespecified result.

## 8. Module and output architecture

### 8.1 `06_analyze_migration_mechanisms.do`

Module 06 will be a single orchestrator because the treatment effects it needs
already exist. It will:

1. verify the protocol, compact analysis registry, six module-04 result files,
   and their manifests;
2. verify the inherited treatment, bandwidth, weighting, clustering, and
   first-stage contracts;
3. extract and classify migration, linkage, and mechanism estimates;
4. run only the new CCPP descriptive-association models;
5. apply multiplicity adjustments within the registered families;
6. export disclosure-safe aggregate CSV, LaTeX, and figure products; and
7. write one output manifest with checksums and
   `review_status=generated_unreviewed`.

The compact registry will live at
`metadata/rd-mechanisms/analysis-registry.csv`. It will contain no observations
and will define the wave, level, variable, denominator, evidence class,
estimand, treatment, sample rule, multiplicity family, and paper role for every
reported row.

Generated products will live only under:

- `output/tables/rd_mechanisms/`
- `output/figures/rd_mechanisms/`
- `metadata/rd-mechanism-output-manifest.csv`

### 8.2 `07_build_tables_figures.do`

Module 07 will not re-estimate models or duplicate artifacts. It will validate
the main-effects, heterogeneity, and mechanism manifests and generate a compact
publication-candidate inventory that points to canonical Git outputs. It may
combine panels only when the component paths and checksums are registered.

All products remain `generated_unreviewed`. Module 07 must not copy anything to
the synchronized Overleaf project; publication sync remains a separate,
explicitly requested review action under `docs/OVERLEAF_WORKFLOW.md`.

### 8.3 `08_run_release_checks.do`

Module 08 will audit, not publish. It will create an aggregate release report
whose status is `PASS` or `BLOCKED`. `BLOCKED` is the correct result when an
artifact is unreviewed or a required disclosure/provenance condition is not
met; it is not converted into a false pipeline success claim.

The release audit must check:

- every registered path exists and has a unique manifest row;
- checksums match the current files;
- generators and input signatures are present;
- no dataset, log, row-level extract, direct identifier, or restricted path is
  registered for release;
- no output uses a prohibited causal-mediation label;
- primary result rows use the registered sample, treatment, bandwidth,
  weighting, clustering, and first-stage gate;
- review status and disclosure status are explicit; and
- Overleaf destinations are blank until a reviewed sync is authorized.

## 9. Failure rules

The workflow must fail with a nonzero Stata return code when an input, key,
schema, design parameter, estimator label, checksum, or expected aggregate
output is missing or inconsistent. It must not weaken a check to finish a run.

Substantive non-identification is handled differently: causal mediation rows
are omitted and the release report records the limitation. Null effects, wide
confidence intervals, failed weak-instrument gates, and `BLOCKED` release
status are valid findings, not software failures.

## 10. Storage, privacy, and publication rules

- No new dataset is written to Git.
- No row-level result, small-cell listing, linkage key, or identifier is
  exported.
- Scratch products use Stata `tempfile`s.
- Existing Dropbox analytical data are read only by modules 06--08.
- Tables and figures are generated first in the canonical Git output folders.
- Every generated artifact is recorded in a non-observation manifest.
- Nothing is copied to Overleaf until separately requested, reviewed, and
  checksum-verified.
- No module invokes a shell, PowerShell, Python, or an external converter.

## 11. Methodological basis

The protocol follows the distinction between total causal effects, effects on
candidate intermediate outcomes, and identified mediation effects. Sequential
ignorability is a substantive identification assumption, not a consequence of
including a mediator in a regression. Natural effects are generally not
identified in the presence of exposure-induced mediator-outcome confounding.
Noncompliance further changes the target population and assumptions for causal
mediation. Sample selection in RD designs also requires explicit assumptions
and cannot be repaired by silently recoding missing outcomes.

Verified methodological sources:

- Imai, Keele, and Tingley (2010), *A General Approach to Causal Mediation
  Analysis*, DOI: <https://doi.org/10.1037/a0020761>.
- VanderWeele, Vansteelandt, and Robins (2014), *Effect Decomposition in the
  Presence of an Exposure-Induced Mediator-Outcome Confounder*, DOI:
  <https://doi.org/10.1097/EDE.0000000000000034>.
- Yamamoto (2013), *Identification and Estimation of Causal Mediation Effects
  with Treatment Noncompliance*, workshop paper:
  <https://appliedstatsworkshopgov3009.hsites.harvard.edu/presentations/identification-and-estimation-causal-mediation-effects>.
- Dippel, Ferrara, and Heblich (2020), *Causal Mediation Analysis in
  Instrumental-Variables Regressions*, DOI:
  <https://doi.org/10.1177/1536867X20953572>.
- Dong (2019), *Regression Discontinuity Designs With Sample Selection*, DOI:
  <https://doi.org/10.1080/07350015.2017.1302880>.
- Lee (2009), *Training, Wages, and Sample Selection: Estimating Sharp Bounds
  on Treatment Effects*, DOI:
  <https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-937X.2009.00536.x>.

These sources justify the restrictions in this protocol; they do not make the
current data sufficient for causal mediation.
