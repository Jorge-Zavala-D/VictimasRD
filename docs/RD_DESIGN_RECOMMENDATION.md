# RD geography and treatment-timing recommendation

## Status

**Recommendation for research-team review; no sample flag has been created.**

This document interprets the expanded first-stage audit generated by
`code/stata/pipeline/03_validate_rd_design.do`. It does not authorize outcome
analysis or replace the remaining validity checks. The proposed decision is:

1. use the **exact legacy geography** as the single common causal RD
   geography, while retaining the national RUV universe for full-sample
   descriptive work;
2. retain **B--C** as the primary cutoff;
3. make the **effect of B-versus-C priority assignment (ITT)** the primary
   causal estimand;
4. define receipt for any fuzzy-RD analysis as **early receipt**, fixed before
   the first outcome wave, rather than changing receipt status with each
   outcome year; and
5. treat `treat_23` as a rollout-catch-up diagnostic rather than a
   sample-selection criterion, and use the planned 2025 Census principally to
   extend the migration analysis under the same geography and early-treatment
   definition.

The exact legacy geography is:

> every RUV community in the departments of Apurímac or Huancavelica, plus
> every RUV community in La Convención province in Cusco or Huancayo province
> in Junín.

This recommendation is conditional on the research team approving the
institutional narrative and the remaining density, score, timing, and
outcome-linkage checks below.

It identifies the most defensible candidate in the current evidence; it does
not guarantee that the final design will support precise causal estimates. If
the remaining validity, linkage, or finite-sample checks fail, the project
should report that the RD design is not viable rather than relax the checks or
search for a more favorable geography.

The seven-department universe is the union of the official five-department
VRAEM envelope and the six departments that the CVR reports account, together
with Ayacucho, for 85 percent of its registered victims. The ten-province
universe is INEI's externally defined VRAEM study envelope. Thus, groups of
three, four, five, and larger cells are fully represented without searching
the unrestricted national province power set. The 1,391 count refers to
reported candidate records across grids; several intentionally overlap, so it
is not a count of unique geographic memberships.

The canonical run records 9,289 first-stage cells and 384 pre-treatment
covariate-continuity cells. The latter cover the 11 predeclared candidates
plus the post-search three-department statistical frontier. All subset
estimates are search diagnostics. Their p-values are not corrected for
selection and cannot justify a sample by themselves.

## Decision criteria

No single statistic chooses the geography. The review uses:

- an institutional definition that existed independently of the new
  estimates;
- positive and stable first-stage direction;
- conventional and robust bias-corrected estimates and uncertainty;
- the descriptive robust Wald-strength diagnostic
  \(W=(\widehat{\tau}_{bc}/SE_{robust})^2\);
- total communities, effective observations, district-cluster support, and
  geographic breadth;
- sensitivity to score-tie rules, bandwidth selection, and polynomial order;
- continuity of 2007 and geographic covariates;
- joint strength in 2012 and 2016, with the complete 2007--2023 path used to
  diagnose rollout dynamics rather than require a 2023 discontinuity; and
- the substantive interpretation of treatment at each outcome date.

Current practical RD guidance suggests treating an F statistic below 20 as a
warning in fuzzy RD applications. The audit's \(W\) is a transparent robust
analogue, not the exact homoskedastic first-stage F statistic and not a
search-adjusted critical value.

No named candidate at any official cutoff has a strong positive 2023 first
stage. No bounded department or province subset has a robust 95 percent
confidence interval above zero at all three B--C horizons, and no candidate
has a strong 2023 B--C first stage. This late-period result is a catch-up
diagnostic, not a requirement that overrides the 2012 and 2016 evidence used
for sample review.

This does not mean every estimated 2023 coefficient is numerically zero.
Across the complete 9,289-cell audit, 68 repeated, search-unadjusted
specification cells have a positive robust confidence interval in 2023.
However, none meets the positive-direction strength heuristic: the largest
2023 robust Wald diagnostic is 8.71, and the most favorable cells are
primarily C--D department combinations discovered through the atlas. They
cannot support a treatment-received fuzzy RD or overturn the predeclared
sample criteria.

The complete annual path is substantively informative. In the exact legacy
geography, the B--C jump rises from 0.024 in 2007 to 0.766 in 2013, peaks near
0.929 in 2017, falls to 0.262 in 2019, and is approximately zero by 2022--2023.
This is rollout catch-up, not evidence that a different late-period geography
should be selected.

## What the higher-order combinations reveal

The higher-order search changes the diagnosis, but not the recommendation.

- The historical Apurímac--Huancavelica core has the strongest 2012
  department result.
- Adding San Martín to that core produces the strongest 2016 result and is the
  most important larger-sample statistical frontier to audit.
  It was identified after the subset search, is geographically disconnected
  from the Andean corridor, and has a very large baseline altitude
  discontinuity.
- Mechanical pair searches also elevate combinations such as
  Apurímac--Cajamarca, Apurímac--Lima, and Apurímac--Piura. Their statistical
  performance and weak common substantive basis demonstrate why the largest
  coefficient cannot define the sample.
- None of the 1,023 official VRAEM province subsets meets the **positive-
  direction** strength criterion in 2012, 2016, or 2023. Several small
  province subsets have large Wald statistics in the wrong direction, and
  some local-polynomial point estimates leave the binary-outcome probability
  range; these are instability warnings, not viable first stages.
- The department results are driven primarily by Apurímac. Selecting
  Apurímac alone after observing this pattern would turn geography into an
  outcome of the search and sharply narrow external validity.

The department power set is equally revealing about scale. Positive first
stages with \(W\geq20\) occur in only three of 127 subsets in 2012 (one each
of sizes one, two, and three) and seven subsets in 2016 (sizes one through
four). None occurs in subsets of five or more departments, and none occurs in
2023 at any subset size. Larger combinations therefore dilute the localized
historical rollout pattern rather than stabilize it.

The search therefore supports a negative conclusion: computational expansion
does not uncover an independently defensible geography with a persistent
contemporaneous-receipt first stage.

## Why the larger three-department frontier does not change the decision

Apurímac, Huancavelica, and San Martín together are the strongest serious
larger-sample alternative revealed by the department power set. The sample
contains 1,464 RUV communities and 608 B--C communities. Its conventional
first-stage estimates are 0.783 through 2012 and 0.956 through 2016, with
robust strength diagnostics of 30.05 and 66.10. The selected bandwidths
contain 73 and 63 observations.

Those advantages do not survive the full decision rule:

- it was identified after inspecting the subset atlas and therefore cannot be
  treated as predeclared;
- the B--C altitude discontinuity is approximately -1,531 meters
  (robust p = 0.005), which is both substantively large and more severe than
  the imbalance in the two-department core;
- San Martín is geographically and topographically distinct from the Andean
  corridor underlying the paper's historical argument; and
- despite adding 302 total RUV communities relative to the exact legacy
  geography, it adds only four effective observations in 2012 and one in
  2016.

It is therefore a transparent post-search diagnostic, not the main sample.
This comparison directly incorporates the requirement for adequate sample
size: geographic breadth matters, but total observations cannot compensate
for local imbalance or post-search selection.

## Why the exact legacy geography is preferred

### Advantages

1. **It predates the new search.** The rule is executable in preserved legacy
   code and is the geography underlying the existing paper.
2. **It has a defensible regional narrative.** Apurímac and Huancavelica are
   high-burden departments surrounding Ayacucho; La Convención and Huancayo
   connect the sample to the formally defined VRAEM conflict corridor.
3. **It exhibits the intended rollout timing.** The B--C first stage is large
   and precise during the 2012--2017 period relevant to the original paper.
4. **It performs better on audited baseline continuity than the smaller
   core.** At B--C, none of eight audited 2007/geographic covariates rejects
   continuity at five percent; the smallest robust p-value is 0.193.
5. **It has more local support than the core.** For `treat_12`, the selected
   bandwidth contains 69 observations in the legacy sample versus 50 in the
   core. The core has only 16 effective observations on the right and its
   bias-corrected binary first-stage estimate slightly exceeds one.
6. **It is not a tiny optimized cell.** It retains 1,162 RUV communities and
   549 B--C communities. Its early/mid effective samples are nearly as large
   as those of the 1,464-community three-department frontier, without that
   frontier's severe altitude discontinuity.

### Limitations

1. The 2012 robust strength diagnostic is 12.16, below the conservative
   heuristic of 20, even though the robust p-value is below 0.001. The 2016
   diagnostic is 22.39.
2. The local effective sample remains small.
3. The exact executable rule and the prose description in the current
   manuscript are not identical. The paper must state the rule exactly.
4. Excluding Ayacucho must be defended with administrative evidence about
   department-level targeting and not merely by its weak first stage.
5. By 2023 the geographic sample has no first stage for contemporaneous
   receipt.

## Why the two-department core is not preferred

The Apurímac--Huancavelica core is a serious sensitivity sample, not the
recommended main sample.

- Its 2012 and 2016 first stages are exceptionally large.
- It is a pre-existing component of the historical rule, not an invented
  national combination.
- However, the effective right-side sample is only 16 communities for the
  2012 estimate.
- Its bias-corrected first-stage estimate is 1.019, an indication of
  local-polynomial instability for a binary outcome in a small local sample.
- Altitude has a robust discontinuity of approximately -404 meters at B--C
  (p = 0.022); the exact legacy geography has no audited covariate rejection.
- Promoting the core only after seeing its stronger jump would be difficult to
  distinguish from first-stage optimization.

It should appear in robustness and sample-sensitivity exhibits, not define the
main results.

## Recommended estimands and timing

### Primary: priority-assignment ITT

The principal estimand should be the discontinuity in outcomes caused by
assignment to the higher-priority B category at the official B--C threshold.
This estimand remains meaningful when later program catch-up weakens the
relationship between priority and cumulative receipt. It also avoids dividing
an outcome discontinuity by a nearly zero 2023 first stage.

### Secondary: early-receipt fuzzy RD

If the team retains a treatment-received estimand, receipt must be fixed before
the first outcome wave:

- use `treat_12` if SISFOH outcomes are measured during 2013 and treatment
  exposure must be complete by 31 December 2012; or
- consider `treat_13` only if the exact SISFOH reference date and CMAN project
  implementation date establish that 2013 projects precede outcome
  measurement.

The same early-receipt definition should then be used for the 2017 and 2025
outcomes. Those estimates concern the medium- and long-run effect of **early
versus delayed receipt**, not treatment versus never treatment.

Fuzzy estimates should use weak-first-stage-robust inference and be presented
as secondary where the strength diagnostic is below 20. The research team
should not use `treat_12`, `treat_16`, and `treat_23` as three interchangeable
treatments: doing so changes the treatment, complier population, and causal
parameter across outcome waves.

### Census 2025

There is no supported fuzzy-RD design for “treated by 2023” in any searched
geography. The planned 2025 Census linkage should therefore be framed
principally as a long-run extension of the migration analysis, not as a new
search across a broad outcome family. It should use:

1. the same exact legacy geography;
2. B--C priority-assignment ITT as the primary estimand; and
3. early receipt as a secondary fixed exposure, interpreted as early versus
   delayed treatment.

If the research question requires the effect of receipt by 2023 specifically,
the current RD design cannot identify it.

## Methodological and institutional anchors

- The [CVR Final Report conclusions](https://www.cverdad.org.pe/ifinal/conclusiones.php)
  identify Ayacucho, Junín, Huánuco, Huancavelica, Apurímac, and San Martín as
  accounting for 85 percent of victims registered by the commission.
- INEI's [VRAEM territorial study](https://sdmr.inei.gob.pe/cms/multimedia/home/menuSect-2-24)
  defines the ten-province envelope used for the complete province-subset
  diagnostic.
- [DS 040-2016-PCM](https://www.minedu.gob.pe/reforma-magisterial/pdf-ley-reforma-magisterial/ds-40-2016-pcm-reordenan-distritos-vraem.pdf)
  defines the direct-intervention and influence districts used to construct
  the predeclared VRAEM proxy candidates.
- Cattaneo, Idrobo, and Titiunik's
  [practical RD introduction](https://rdpackages.github.io/references/Cattaneo-Idrobo-Titiunik_2024_CUP.pdf)
  motivates explicit first-stage diagnostics and an ITT interpretation when
  the fuzzy first stage is weak.

## Required checks before creating `sample_main_rd`

1. **Team approval and versioned decision.** Approve the exact geography,
   B--C cutoff, score-tie rule, and primary/secondary estimands.
2. **Outcome dates.** Verify the SISFOH 2013 and Census 2017 reference dates and
   obtain the 2025 Census timing and linkage protocol.
3. **Administrative timing.** Confirm whether the CMAN year is approval,
   financing, initiation, completion, or delivery.
4. **Score reconstruction.** Complete the audit of the official victimization
   formula and the out-of-range scores.
5. **Running-variable diagnostics.** Repair the local `rddensity`
   installation (`rddensity_fun.do` is missing), assess whether density tests
   are appropriate for the rounded mass-point score, and complement them with
   local-randomization diagnostics.
6. **Covariate validity.** Review the complete 384-cell continuity output,
   multiplicity, bandwidth sensitivity, and joint randomization tests.
7. **Finite-sample inference.** Report effective observations and clusters,
   use appropriate cluster and weak-first-stage procedures, and test
   sensitivity to the clustering level.
8. **Outcome linkage and attrition.** Re-run sample-flow, match-rate, and
   continuity checks separately for SISFOH, Census 2017, and Census 2025.
9. **Spatial interference.** Assess spillovers and geographic dependence
   around the selected communities.
10. **Manuscript consistency.** Replace the current approximate prose with the
    exact executable geographic rule and explain why it is fixed across all
    outcomes.

Until these checks and team approval are complete,
`03_validate_rd_design.do` must continue to avoid writing `sample_main_rd`.
